    Liner notes

    We went to the coast to lose the signal.

    The boathouse had no heating and a radio that only picked up two stations: the shipping forecast and, after midnight, something in a language none of us could place. We left it on while we recorded. Half of the room sounds on this album are that radio, bleeding through the floorboards.

    We'd written the songs in London in a hurry, the way you write when you're trying to prove something. The sea slowed them down. Songs that were four minutes became six. The drums got quieter. Marguerite started playing the harmonium we found under a tarp, and by the third day it was on everything.

    This is a record about the hour before the tide turns, when everything is very still and you can't yet tell which way it's going to go.

    "We stopped fixing the mistakes around the second week. They were the only honest thing in the room."
