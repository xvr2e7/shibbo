How we plant

Planting is the easy Saturday. Keeping it alive is the job.

Most street trees that die do it in their first three summers, from thirst. So we built the whole organization around the watering can.

  1. The block asks

    We don't pick streets from a map. A resident requests trees, we knock on every door together, and we go ahead once six in ten households sign on.

  2. We open the sidewalk

    Our crew cuts tree pits at least four by ten feet and swaps rubble for real soil. A big tree in a small hole is a slow way to lose a tree.

  3. Neighbors plant it

    On fall Saturdays, volunteers set the root ball, stake, mulch and tag each tree. No experience needed; we bring gloves, shovels and coffee.

  4. Three years of water

    Every tree gets a named steward on the block and twenty gallons a week from May through September. Our cargo trikes cover the gaps.

Where we plant

Shade isn't evenly shared.

Halden's leafiest neighborhood has more than six times the tree cover of its barest, and on a July afternoon you can feel the difference through your shoes. We work only where canopy is under 15%.

What we plant

Right tree, right pit.

A street is a hard place to be a tree: road salt, compacted soil, overhead wires. We plant a mix of species that can take it, so one pest can't clear a block.

Swamp white oak

Quercus bicolor

Handles soil that floods in April and bakes in August. Our most-planted tree.
