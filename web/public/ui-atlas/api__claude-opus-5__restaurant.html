Our Story

We opened with
four tables and
one mortar & pestle.

In 1987 my parents, Mariam and Fouad, arrived with two suitcases, a sack of dried mint, and a copper pot that had already fed three generations in Zahlé. They rented a narrow room with a broken awning and started frying falafel at six in the morning.

Nothing much has changed. The mint still hangs drying in the back hallway. My brother still argues with me about how much garlic belongs in the toum. The charcoal still gets lit at four o'clock, and the first guest still gets a plate of olives they didn't ask for.

Tfaddalo — come in, sit down, eat too much. That's the whole idea.

Nadia KhourySecond generation · Head of the kitchen
