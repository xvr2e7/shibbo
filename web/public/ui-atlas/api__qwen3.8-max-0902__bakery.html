02 — The process

Two days, four hands,
one very old jar.

Every loaf on the board started life 48 hours before you ate it. Long, cold and slow is the whole trick — there is no shortcut we haven't tried and regretted.

Scroll — the dough rises as you do ↓
  1. Thu · 17:00

    Gerald gets fed

    Our nine-year-old starter takes rye, water and breakfast. He doubles by evening and smells faintly of green apples.

  2. Thu · 21:30

    The mix

    Stoneground flour from Fell Hollow Mill, water, salt, Gerald. That's the whole ingredient list, for every loaf, forever.

  3. Thu · 22:15

    Folds, then bed

    Four sets of folds over two hours, then the trays head to the cold room at 4°C for a long, slow, flavour-building night.

  4. Fri · 05:40

    Shape & score

    Cold dough out, bench flour down, lames at the ready. Every loaf gets the same crooked slash — our signature, our scar.

  5. Fri · 06:10

    Into the oven

    260°C, steam, stone. The bake smells of toast and honey and drifts roughly halfway down the lane by 6:40.

  6. Fri · 07:00

    Doors open

    First come, first loaf. This is the bit that goes fast — and the reason this page exists. See you in the queue.

06 — Fair questions

Asked at the counter

One oven, four pairs of hands, zero freezer. We bake exactly what we can do brilliantly — 248 loaves is that number. Bake more and we'd bake worse, and you'd know, because you always know.

Honestly: no. The shelves are bare, the till is counted, and somebody is eating toast on the step. Occasionally a bag of Misfits appears around 4pm — follow @odd.loaf for the bat-signal.

Flattered, but no. Every gram of our flour goes to the lane, not to a van. If a café near you serves Odd Loaf, someone is reselling it and we would very much like a word.

Wild-fermented for around 24 hours with Gerald as the only leaven — no commercial yeast, no improvers, no ascorbic acid tricks. Long fermentation is also why most of us find it gentler on the stomach.

The starter. Nine years old, fed twice daily, bubblier than most of us before coffee. Gerald has outlived two bakers' phones, one flour supplier and the great fridge failure of 2022. Long may he rise.
