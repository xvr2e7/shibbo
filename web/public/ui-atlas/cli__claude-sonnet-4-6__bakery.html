Our Story

Odd by name,
not by nature.

We started in a garage with one dutch oven, a sourdough starter named Gerald, and a frankly unreasonable number of library books about fermentation.

Now we bake out of a small unit on Kellett Street, using stone-milled heritage grains from farms within a day's drive. Every loaf is mixed by hand, cold-proofed overnight, and baked at 5am so you can walk out with something warm.

We're small on purpose. When the bread is gone, it's gone.

The Method

Slow bread is better bread.

Everything we bake follows the same unhurried logic. There are no shortcuts, and we're not interested in finding them.

01
Milling & Mixing
We source grain from two farms in the region and have it milled fresh each week. Flours are weighed and mixed by hand on the previous evening, with our starter, water, and a little salt. Nothing else.
02
Bulk Fermentation
The dough ferments at room temperature for 4–6 hours, with gentle folds every 30 minutes. We watch the dough, not the clock — temperature, humidity, and the starter's mood all factor in.
03
Shaping & Cold Proof
Each loaf is shaped by hand and placed in a floured banneton. It goes into the fridge overnight — anywhere from 10 to 16 hours depending on the dough. Cold proofing deepens the flavour and makes for a better ear.
04
Baking at Dawn
The oven goes on at 4:30am. We bake in cast iron at 250°C, lid on for steam, lid off to colour. Each loaf is scored with a lame just before it goes in. First batch out by 6:45am, second by 8:30am.
