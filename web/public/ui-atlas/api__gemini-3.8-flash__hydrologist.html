Station: McKenzie River at Clear Lake (44°21'N, 121°59'W) • Temp: 4.8°C • Discharge: 412 cfs (stable baseflow)
Rivers are not corridors through landscapes; they are the landscape dreaming in motion, carrying the crushed mountains of three epochs toward an insatiable sea.

I am a catchment hydrologist and writer. For fifteen years I have measured gravel displacement, channel migration, and thermal stratification across cold-water rivers. Between acoustic Doppler surveys, I write long essays exploring how moving water constructs our geology, our memory, and our legal fictions.

Fieldwork & Biography

Curriculum Vitae

I am an Associate Researcher in Watershed Sciences at Oregon State University and an affiliate fellow at the River Basin Center. I received my doctorate in Fluvial Geomorphology from the University of British Columbia, where my dissertation investigated bedload transport rates during extreme snowmelt runoff in boulder-bed mountain torrents.

My research relies on both acoustic instrumentation (ADCP, hydrophones listening to gravel clashing underwater) and prolonged, patient physical observation. I am especially interested in hyporheic exchange—the porous, invisible zone beneath riverbeds where surface water and deep groundwater intermingle.

My literary essays have appeared in Orion Magazine, Ecotone, The Georgia Review, and Terrain.org, and have been anthologized in The Best American Science & Nature Writing. My first book of essays, tentatively titled The Silt Ledger: Geologic Time on Running Water, will be published by Graywolf Press in late 2026.
