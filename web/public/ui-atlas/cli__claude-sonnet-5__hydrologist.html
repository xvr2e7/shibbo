Notes from the water's edge

About

I'm a hydrologist based in the upper watershed of the Kettleford River, where I've spent the better part of fifteen years measuring discharge, mapping floodplains, and trying to understand what a river actually wants. My research has focused on sediment transport and channel migration, but most of what I know about rivers I've learned by walking their banks slower than I probably should.

This site collects essays written alongside that work — not papers, but the thinking that doesn't fit in a journal: what it means to read a gravel bar, why a river's memory outlasts its banks, and what gets lost when we straighten a channel for convenience.

I hold a PhD in Earth Sciences and currently split my time between fieldwork, consulting on river restoration projects, and writing.
