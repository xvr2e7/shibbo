    We didn't set out to make a night record. The night just kept showing up.
    — June Okafor, vocals & guitar

    After two years of touring their debut Paper Weather, Velvet Moth retreated to a friend's farmhouse in upstate New York with a four-track, a broken Wurlitzer and no plan. Sleep didn't come easily. So they stopped fighting it.

    Small Hours was tracked almost entirely after midnight, with the windows open — you can hear crickets on “Lantern Song” and a passing freight train at the end of “Wide Awake, Mostly.” It's their warmest, strangest and most patient record yet.

    Produced by the band with Theo Marsh at Hollow Barn, mixed in Philadelphia, pressed on vinyl in Nashville.
