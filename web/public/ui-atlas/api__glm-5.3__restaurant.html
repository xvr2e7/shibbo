Our Story · قصتنا

In 1974, Em Khalil opened four tables on a side street in Beirut and served whatever her garden gave that morning. Nothing about that has changed — except that her grandchildren now argue over who makes better kibbeh.

Every vine leaf is still rolled by hand. The parsley is still chopped twice — once for tabbouleh, once to settle the argument about how it should be done. We measure time in generations, not seasons, and we’d rather run out of a dish than rush it.

When you eat here, you eat with us. There is no such thing as a quick bite at Bayt Sitti — there is only a long table, and you are welcome at it.

إميليا خليل Emilia Khalil — the granddaughter who took over the kitchen
  • 1974

    Four tables on a side street

    Em Khalil starts cooking from her home kitchen in Beirut. Neighbours become regulars within a month.

  • 1989

    The wood oven is built

    Her son Georges bricks the manoushé oven by hand. It hasn’t gone cold since.

  • 2011

    A second house

    The family opens this kitchen abroad — same recipes, same arguments, same garden herbs grown on the roof.

  • Today

    Three generations, one table

    Sitti Em is 91 and still inspects every tray of warak enab. We have learned not to argue with her.
