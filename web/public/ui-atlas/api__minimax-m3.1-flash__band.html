The story

A record about
staying up late.

“Neon Hymn” is the third Lantern Parade album and the first one we wrote entirely together in a room. It is softer than the last record and louder than we expected.

It started with a broken reverb unit that could only hum one note. We kept it. Everything you hear in the first four minutes is that hum, fed through a plate reverb and a four-track that we found in a closing sale.

Most of the album was written between 2am and 5am, which explains the title track's chorus and the general atmosphere of a city that has stopped pretending to be asleep. Sol and I traded verses over voice notes until the sun came up and then again the next night.

It is our first record with a full band on it — Jonah on drums, Priya on bass — and the first time we let a song run past four minutes. We hope that means something.

    Words from the sleeve

    Printed in the booklet, read aloud by nobody. Reproduced here with permission (and mild regret).

    Track 01 — Paper Cathedrals

    We built a church out of receipts

    and lit it with the coffee machine,

    and every hymn was a hum

    the heating left behind.

    Track 03 — Lantern Parade

    Carry it gently, it is not a light,

    it is a small persistent noise,

    a reason to walk in the same direction

    until the streetlights run out.

    Track 10 — Neon Hymn

    Sing it in sodium, sing it in rain,

    the tune is a key that fits any door

    and every window that remembers us

    is brighter than it was before.
