Anatomy

Four decisions you can taste.

Nothing here is decoration. Each line of the section drawing is a choice about how fast water leaves the bed, and how much of the coffee it takes with it.

  • 01

    A 60° wall

    Steeper than a basket, gentler than a V. The bed forms a deep cone, so water passes through more coffee per millilitre and the extraction stays even from top to bottom.

  • 02

    Twenty-four spiral ribs

    They hold the paper a hair off the wall. Air escapes down the channels instead of pinning the filter shut, which keeps the flow steady from first pour to last drip.

  • 03

    One wide drip hole

    38 mm, unrestricted. The grind and your pour set the pace — not the cone. Coarser for sweetness and body, finer for clarity and lift.

  • 04

    A body that holds heat

    Four millimetres of dense stoneware. Rinse it with hot water and the cone stops stealing degrees from your brew — the difference between flat and sweet in the last 60 ml.

The house recipe

22 g in, 360 g out,
three minutes flat.

A starting point, not a rule. Medium grind, water at 94 °C, filter rinsed and the cone warmed through.

0:00 — 0:45

Bloom

Pour 50 g in a slow spiral and stop. Let the bed swell and breathe off its gas. Give it the full forty-five seconds.

0:45 — 1:30

First build

Up to 200 g, centre outward, never touching the paper. Keep the bed submerged but calm.

1:30 — 2:10

Second build

Finish at 360 g in two gentle pulses. A short swirl of the cone settles the grounds flat.

2:10 — 3:00

Drawdown

Let it fall. Dry by three minutes means you're close; much slower, grind coarser tomorrow.

The kiln

“Clay remembers the hand that moved it. We only ask it to hold heat and let go of water.”

Mariko Hoshino · fourth-generation potter, Tokoname

1894KILN ESTABLISHED
11HANDS PER CONE
2×FIRINGS

Tokoname has been firing clay for nine hundred years, mostly for teapots. The same iron-rich body that makes a kyusu pour cleanly makes a dripper that holds its temperature — dense, slightly sandy, forgiving in the kiln.

Each cone is thrown on a wheel, trimmed the next morning, then bisque fired. The ribs are pressed from a single mould so the spiral runs true; a crooked rib channels water to one side, and you can taste it. After glazing, a second firing to 1,250 °C vitrifies the body so it will never take on the flavour of the coffee before it.

Small variations in glaze thickness and colour are a record of the kiln's hot and cool corners. We don't sort them out. A cone with no variation would mean no one was there.

Questions

Before you order.

Which size should I buy?
If you usually brew a single mug, take the 01 — a small bed in a large cone extracts unevenly. The 02 handles 15 g to 40 g of coffee comfortably, so choose it if you ever brew for two. Most people are happier with the 02.
Do I really need to preheat it?
Yes, and it's the one step that changes the cup most. Ceramic this dense will pull several degrees out of your water if it starts cold. Rinse the filter with hot water, let the cone sit for twenty seconds, discard the water, then brew.
Will it fit my carafe or server?
The foot is 100 mm across and sits on any opening between 55 mm and 95 mm. The handle projects 38 mm beyond the rim, so check the clearance if your server has a narrow neck or a tall lid.
What filters does it take?
Any standard 02 cone paper — bleached or natural, tabbed or not. A hundred natural filters ship in the box. Cloth and reusable metal cones in the 02 shape also seat correctly on the ribs.
What if it arrives broken?
We replace it, no photographs or forms required. The same goes for any breakage in the first year of ownership, including the kind that is entirely your fault. Just write to us.
Is the glaze safe and dishwasher proof?
The glaze is food-safe and free of lead and cadmium, and the body is fully vitrified, so it is dishwasher safe. Avoid thermal shock: don't pour boiling water into a cone straight from the fridge, and don't put it over direct heat.
