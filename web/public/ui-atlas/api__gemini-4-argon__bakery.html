Why We Close at Noon

Bread takes thirty-six hours. Selling it takes four.

People often knock on the glass at 1:15 PM asking why we don't "just bake another batch for the afternoon." We wish we could.

Every loaf on our pine shelves was mixed two days ago, folded by hand in ceramic tubs, and fermented slowly at 4°C so wild lactobacilli can break down the gluten and unlock deep, nutty sweetness without sour harshness.

House Philosophy

Why we refuse to buy a mechanical bread slicer.

A high-hydration sourdough loaf is still finishing its bake for two hours after leaving the oven. Slicing a warm or freshly cooled boule in a 28-blade machine vents all its moisture and turns a 5-day loaf into day-old toast by suppertime.

Keep your loaf cut-side down on a wooden board—no plastic bags, ever. The crust acts as its own Dutch oven.
