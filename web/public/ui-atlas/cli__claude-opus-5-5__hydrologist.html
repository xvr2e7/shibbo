About

I'm a hydrologist. I spend most of my working life measuring rivers: how fast they rise, how much they carry, where they're likely to go next. I studied fluvial geomorphology, then spent a decade on flood-risk modelling for public agencies before splitting my time between research and writing.

The essays here are where the data ends and the questions begin. They're about the physics of moving water, but also about the towns, farms, and ecosystems that have bet their futures on a river behaving. I write for anyone who has stood on a bridge and wondered where all that water is going.

I live near a small, flood-prone creek, which keeps me honest.
