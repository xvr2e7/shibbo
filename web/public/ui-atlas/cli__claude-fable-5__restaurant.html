Our Story

From Teta's Kitchen to Yours

When our grandmother Marie left the village of Douma in 1986, she carried two suitcases: one of clothes, and one of spices, dried mint, and a hand-written notebook of recipes. A year later, Beit Khoury — "the Khoury house" — opened its doors with six tables and one rule: no one leaves hungry.

Today her son Elias runs the kitchen, her granddaughter Nour runs the dining room, and the notebook still sits on the shelf above the stove. We grind our own spices, roll warqa by hand, simmer our hummus chickpeas overnight, and bake man'oushe to order on the saj — the same way it was done in Douma.

This is not fusion. This is not a concept. This is our family's table, and there is always room for you at it.

— The Khoury Family

Three Generations

1986

1987

Beit Khoury opens with six tables, a borrowed oven, and a line out the door by spring.

2004

Elias takes over the kitchen; the saj oven is installed and man'oushe joins the menu.

2019

Nour, third generation, joins full-time and opens the vine-covered back terrace.

Today

Same notebook. Same rule. No one leaves hungry.
