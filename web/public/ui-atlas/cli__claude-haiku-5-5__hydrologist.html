About

I am a hydrologist. For most of my career I have measured streamflow, studied sediment transport, and tried to explain why a river behaves the way it does on a given Tuesday in April. I work with gauges, models, and long records, and I have learned that the most useful thing a river can teach is patience.

I write essays about what I see along the way: floods that arrive later than expected, channels that remember old courses, and the people who depend on water they rarely think about. These pieces are my attempt to make hydrology legible to readers who have never looked closely at a riverbank.

Thanks for reading.
