The problem

A city block without trees is a different climate.

Asphalt and rooftops hold heat long after sunset. On the same afternoon, in the same city, a bare street can run 15–20°F hotter than one with a full canopy — and the hottest blocks are almost always the ones with the least tree cover and the fewest resources to change that.

  • Heat is the deadliest weather hazard in the country, and it lands hardest on older adults, outdoor workers, and kids.
  • A mature street tree intercepts thousands of gallons of stormwater a year, easing pressure on aging sewers.
  • Shaded sidewalks get walked. Tree-lined streets see slower traffic, cooler bus stops, and more neighbors outside.

What we do

Four programs, one promise: the tree is still alive in ten years.

Planting is the easy part. Most urban trees die young from drought, damage, or neglect in their first three summers — so every tree we put in comes with a decade of care attached to it.

Street tree planting

Resident-requested trees in sidewalk pits, medians, and bus-stop corners. We handle permits, species selection, soil work, and the planting crew — you pick the spot.

Our core

Ten-year care

Watering routes through the dry months, structural pruning in years three and six, mulch rings, stake removal, and replacement of any tree we lose. Tracked tree by tree.

Schools

Schoolyard shade

Depaving blacktop playgrounds and replacing it with shade trees, benches, and rain gardens — designed with the students who use them at recess.

Tree steward training

A free six-week course in pruning, pest ID, soil health, and advocacy. Graduates lead their own block's plantings — 6,300 trained and counting.

Heat mapping & data

Volunteers walk routes with sensors each August. The resulting canopy and temperature atlas is public, and it decides where we plant next.

Policy & protection

We push for canopy ordinances, bigger tree pits in street redesigns, and funded maintenance budgets — because a city can't volunteer its way to shade.

Impact

Our goal is 30% canopy in every neighborhood. No exceptions.
