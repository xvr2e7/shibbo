03 · The hydrologist

A field biography

Portrait, bio & core sample · taken at the weir

I’m Nora Calder, a hydrologist who measures rivers for a living and writes essays to explain to myself why it matters. By day I keep a network of gauging stations in the Pennines — stage boards, current meters, a temperamental flume — and by night I turn the day’s readings into sentences. Measurement is the body of the work; writing is how it breathes.

Fifteen years of fieldwork have taken me from storm readings on the Dee at two in the morning to six weeks of glacier-melt pulse on Iceland’s Skeiðarársandur. My essays have appeared in Orion, Emergence and The Commonweal, and my first collection, Baseflow: A Year at the Weir, came out in 2019. I hold a research fellowship at the Institute of River Sciences.

I live in Hebden Bridge within earshot of the Calder, with a rack of drying waders and an unreasonable number of notebooks. This site is my open ledger: every essay gauged, dated and filed. Come down to the water sometime — the reading is better close up.
