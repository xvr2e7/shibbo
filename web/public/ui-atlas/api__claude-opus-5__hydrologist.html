About

For eighteen years I have worked on rivers in the Columbia Basin, first as a field technician wading the Kootenai with a pygmy meter, later as a watershed hydrologist writing the flow models that decide when a reservoir is drawn down and who gets water in August. The job is mostly patience: a stilling well, a rating curve, a sediment core, the long argument between rainfall and rock.

The essays began as field notes. They are still, in some sense, field notes — attempts to say what a hydrograph cannot: that a river is an event rather than a thing, that the hundred-year flood is a habit of thought, that restoration is a form of apology written in gravel.

I live in Hood River, Oregon, within earshot of a creek that disappears every July and comes back without explanation.
