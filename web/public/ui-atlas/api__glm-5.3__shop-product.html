01The Craft
Hudiksvall, Sweden · Est. 2019

Thrown, not molded.

Every K-1 starts as a nine-hundred-gram wedge of clay — iron-flecked, dense, unglamorous until fire gets at it.

We throw each cone in one piece on a jiggered foot, then cut the twelve spiral ribs by hand while the clay is still soft. They aren't decoration: they lift the paper off the wall, so water runs instead of seeping.

The walls go eight millimetres thick on purpose. All that clay drinks in the heat of your kettle water and pays it back to the slurry for the whole brew — which is the quiet argument for ceramic over glass or steel. Your water never notices the pour.

03The Ritual
Logged at the bar · 30 g dose · #02 paper

Four minutes, done properly.

  1. Wet the whole bed with twice the coffee's weight in water — 60 g on a 30 g dose. Then stop. Watch it swell and exhale: that's CO₂ leaving, and bitterness leaving with it. Forty-five seconds, hands in pockets.

  2. Start at the center and spiral outward, low and slow, until the scale reads roughly half your water weight. Keep the bed level — you're rinsing, not excavating.

  3. Two steady pulses down the same spiral, topping up to full weight. This isn't to agitate — it's to rinse the grounds off the wall and back into the conversation.

  4. Hands off. A flat, even bed at the end means an even extraction. The last drops are the sweetest — let them land, then lift the cone and smell the spent grounds. That's your note for tomorrow.
