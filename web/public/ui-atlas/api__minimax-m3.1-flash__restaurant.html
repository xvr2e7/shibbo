Our story

It started with a wooden table and a stubborn cook.

In 1962, Salma Al Wazzan set up two tables in the front room of her family's flat on Maronite Street. She cooked what she knew — kibbeh, fattoush, a charcoal grill borrowed from a cousin — and fed whoever came through the door.

Sixty-two years and three generations later, the recipes have not changed. The bakers still grind the sumac by hand. The bread still comes out of the oven at noon and again at six. And on Friday, the mezze table is still laid the way Salma laid it.

1962 — Two tables in Gemmayzeh

Salma opens a tiny kitchen serving home cooking to neighbours, artists and taxi drivers.

1989 — The charcoal grill arrives

Grandfather Khalil joins the business and the taouk becomes the dish everyone orders twice.

2016 — Third generation, same door

Rami and Layla take over, keeping the same menu, the same welcome, and the same kitchen.

“If the guests leave with full stomachs and full hearts, we did our job. That has been the recipe for sixty years.” — Salma Al Wazzan, founder

Shish Taouk, the way Khalid liked it

  • Marinated a full 24 hours in our own garlic-lemon marinade
