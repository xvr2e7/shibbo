About the Record

01 / Story

Formed in 2017 in a rented garage outside Asheville, The Hollow Pines have spent the better part of a decade chasing a sound that feels both old and unfinished. Their first two records were built on demo tapes and stubborn optimism. Static Bloom was built on something harder to come by: time.

Frontwoman and songwriter Mara Lind wrote most of the album during a winter of long nights and short days, bringing fragments to the band that were reshaped in a sweltering rehearsal room. Guitarist Eli Porter layered the textures, drummer Sam Oduya kept everything grounded, and bassist Jonah Reyes tied it together.

Produced by the band and mixed at a small studio on the river, the album leans into the static between things — the hiss before a signal comes through, the moment a chorus blooms out of silence.

It was recorded live to tape with minimal overdubs, and it sounds like four people in a room who mean every word.

"It's the sound of trying to tune in to something real and finally getting through." Mara Lind, on the record
