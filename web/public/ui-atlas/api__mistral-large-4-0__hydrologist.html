02 About

I spent fifteen years as a field hydrologist — a job that is mostly standing in rivers in bad weather, holding an instrument and writing down what the water is doing. I measured discharge on the Mekong, tracked sediment on the Mississippi, weighed snowpacks in the Idaho backcountry, and once spent a flood night on a levee at 3 A.M. filling a soggy notebook with numbers.

In 2021 I left the gauge stations to write full-time. My essays are about what the measurements mean: the flood, the drought, the dam, the delta, the ferry crossing, the color of the water. They have appeared in The Long Current, Fieldnotes Quarterly, and the anthology Water Marks (2023). I am at work on a book called High Water.

I live in Portland, Oregon, within earshot of the Willamette, with a rain gauge, a dog named Eddy, and a growing conviction that most people look at rivers without seeing them. This site is where I practice showing them.
