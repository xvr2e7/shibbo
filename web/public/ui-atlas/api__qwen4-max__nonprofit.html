Why city trees

Shade is infrastructure.

Low-canopy neighborhoods can run up to 12°F hotter than leafy ones. A single street tree is a public-health tool, a storm drain, and a gathering place all at once.

Cooler streets

Tree shade lowers surface temperatures on pavement and walls, easing dangerous summer heat islands.

−12°Fpeak surface cooling

Cleaner air

Leaves filter particulate pollution from traffic and absorb CO₂ year after year.

48 lbsCO₂ absorbed / tree / year

Less flooding

Roots and canopy intercept rainfall, keeping stormwater out of overwhelmed sewers.

1,500 galstormwater captured / year

Healthier people

Greener blocks are linked to lower stress, more walking, and stronger neighborhood ties.

How it works

From donation to shade in three steps.

1

We listen & map

Residents nominate streets. We combine their input with heat and canopy data to prioritize the blocks with the greatest need and the best planting sites.

2

We plant together

On community planting days, volunteers plant nursery-grown native species chosen for each site — with the property owner's blessing and a species they help pick.

3

We care for 3 years

Every tree is assigned a trained steward and logged in our open tree map. We water through summers, mulch each spring, and prune for strong structure.

Good things to know.

Yes! Any resident can nominate a planting site through our volunteer form or by email. We review nominations each season against heat data, utility clearances, and sidewalk width — and we always confirm with the adjacent property owner before planting.

87% funds trees, tools, and stewardship directly; 13% keeps the lights on. Every donated tree is added to our public tree map with its species, planting date, and steward, so you can literally watch your gift grow. We publish audited financials every year.

None at all. Planting days start with a 20-minute hands-on demo, and crew leaders guide every step. If digging isn't your thing, we also need waterers, photographers, translators, and people who are great at handing out lemonade.

Mostly regionally native species — oaks, hackberries, serviceberries, redbuds, and swamp white oaks in wet sites — chosen with our city arborist for climate resilience, mature size, and low allergen impact. Residents help pick from a shortlist suited to their site.

Each tree gets a trained volunteer steward plus our professional care crew for three years: weekly summer watering, spring mulch, and structural pruning. After year three, trees join the city's protected street-tree inventory. That commitment is why 94% of our trees survive.
