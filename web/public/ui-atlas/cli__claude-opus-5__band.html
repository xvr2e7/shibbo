01 — The Record

Songs about staying put while everything keeps moving.

Mixed to ½″ tape · Lorain, OH · 2026

Three years after Weather for Nobody, Swim Month came back with no songs and a borrowed key to a room above a laundromat. The dryers ran until nine every night, so the band tracked after close — which is why half of this album hums at a low B flat if you listen for it.

Static Bloom is about the strange arithmetic of a place you didn't leave: the friends who did, the ones who came back, the radio tower blinking over the lake like it's counting something. The guitars are louder than before. The songs are shorter, except when they refuse to be.

Nothing here was fixed in a computer. Takes end where hands got tired. The pedal steel on "Low Tide Choir" is one pass, recorded at 4 a.m. through a guitar amp with a broken reverb tank, and you can hear the spring settle at the end of it.

If the first record was about getting out, this one is what happens when you stop trying — and find out the place grew around you anyway. Something static. Something blooming.
