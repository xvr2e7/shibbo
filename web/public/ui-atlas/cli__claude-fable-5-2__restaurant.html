Our story

A kitchen that started with one pot and a long table.

“If someone walks in while we are eating, we don’t ask if they’re hungry. We bring a plate.”
— Teta Noura Haddad, founder

Noura Haddad left her village in the hills above Zahlé with a suitcase, a brass coffee pot, and her mother’s recipes written on the backs of envelopes. In 1987 she opened a six-table lunch counter and cooked everything herself: the lentils, the stuffed vine leaves, the bread.

Today her son Elias tends the charcoal, her granddaughter Maya runs the dining room, and Teta still arrives every morning to taste the hummus before anyone else is allowed to. The recipes haven’t changed. The envelopes are framed by the door.

We cook the way we cook at home: olive oil from a family grove in the Koura, lemons squeezed to order, parsley chopped by hand because a machine bruises it. Nothing is rushed, and nobody leaves hungry.

Beyond dinner

More ways to eat with us.

Sunday family lunch

Every Sunday from noon, Teta’s weekly special comes out of the big pot. When it’s gone, it’s gone. Children under six eat as our guests.

Catering & gatherings

Weddings, baptisms, office lunches, and everything in between. Mezze trays and whole roasted lamb for ten to two hundred guests.

The pantry shelf

Take a little of the kitchen home: our za’atar blend, Koura olive oil, pickled turnips, toum by the jar, and bread still warm in the bag.
