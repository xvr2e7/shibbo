Please read

How to actually get bread.

We get asked the same three questions every morning, so here they are — answered once, in writing, with love.

01

Come before ten

Half the bake is gone by 09:30. If you want the Olive & Thyme or a croissant, set an alarm. Weekends are busier than weekdays by about an hour.

02

Take a paper ticket

The blue tin by the door. It keeps things fair and it stops the queue from blocking the pavement for Mrs. Aziz next door, which she appreciates.

03

Bring a bag, if you can

We'll always give you paper, but a cloth bag keeps the crust crackling all the way home. No holds, no phone orders, no exceptions — not even for our mums.

The method

Four ingredients.
Thirty-six hours.

There is no improver, no yeast, no shortcut and no overnight dough from a factory. Our starter is called Gladys. She is nine years old, lives in a battered Kilner jar, and has been fed every single day since 2016 — including the day the pipes froze.

The flour comes from a mill two counties over. The salt is from Cheshire. The water is from the tap. Everything else is patience.
