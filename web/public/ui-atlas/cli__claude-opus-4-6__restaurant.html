Our family, your table

Bayti — meaning "my home" in Arabic — began when our grandmother Layla started cooking for neighbors out of her apartment in 1987. Word spread quickly: the hummus was smoother, the tabbouleh brighter, the kibbeh crispier.

Today her grandchildren run the kitchen, but the recipes haven't changed. We still hand-roll every grape leaf, slow-roast our shawarma overnight, and bake our flatbread fresh every morning. Every dish carries a story, and every meal is an invitation to be part of our family.

— The Khoury Family
