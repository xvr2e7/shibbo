How we bake

Slow dough, small oven, slightly odd shapes.

Odd Loaf started as a Saturday thing out of a flat window. Then a trestle table. Then the lease on the old launderette on Pilgrim Street, which still has the drain in the floor.

We never scaled up because we didn't want to. Two of us, one deck oven, and a starter named Gerald that's been going since 2019. Everything's mixed the afternoon before, shaped by hand, proved overnight in the cold room and baked while the street's still dark.

"No loaf is the same shape twice. That's not a flaw, that's a signature."

  1. Feed Gerald, 2pm

    The starter gets flour and water and a few hours to think about it.

  2. Mix & fold, 5pm

    Stoneground flour from a mill two counties over. Four folds, by hand, over three hours.

  3. Shape & chill, 8pm

    Into baskets, into the cold room. Thirty-six hours of slow fermentation does the real work.

  4. Bake, 5am

    Scored, steamed, baked dark. The crust should crackle when you squeeze it. If it doesn't, we didn't.

How to actually get a loaf

We're genuinely sorry about the queue. We're also not making more. Here's how to improve your odds.

Reserve the night before

Text us by 8pm with your name and what you want. We hold it until 10am, then it goes back on the shelf. Max two loaves per person, because fairness.

Text 07700 900123

Come early, bring coffee

There's often a short queue from 6:45. The café two doors down opens at 6:30 and knows exactly why you're there. Buns are gone by 8:30 on Saturdays. Don't say we didn't warn you.

Standing order

Want the same loaf every week without thinking? Set up a standing order and we'll put your name on a basket. Pay monthly, pause whenever, collect by 11.
