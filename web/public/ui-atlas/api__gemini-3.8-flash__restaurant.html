Three Generations of Passion

"In Lebanon, the table is sacred. No stranger leaves without being fed."

Beituna began in 1978 when Teta Leila and Amo Farah arrived with little more than a vintage brass mortar, a satchel of dry mountain za'atar, and family recipes guarded for over a century.

Today, the kitchen is run by their sons and grandchildren. While the chairs have evolved, nothing about the food has changed. The mint is picked each morning, our labneh hangs in cheesecloth overnight, and our kibbeh is still pounded with love and pine nuts.

The Grand Lebanese "Taula"

Every Sunday afternoon, we honor the village tradition of the unending table. An uninterrupted flow of 12 cold and hot mezze, mixed grill skewers fresh off the coals, warm pita continuously brought from the saj, finishing with mint tea and warm knefeh.
