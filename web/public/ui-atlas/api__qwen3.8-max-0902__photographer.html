02 / The Shape of a Day

Eight hours,held lightly.

Every wedding has the same bones and none of them alike. I work in long, unhurried stretches — close enough to see what happens, quiet enough that it does. Here is how a typical Saturday unfolds in my viewfinder.

Keep scrolling — the day moves as you do ↓

  1. 2:00 pm

    Details & getting ready

    The dress on a hook, steam rising off the kettle, a grandmother's earrings finding a collarbone. The day is mostly made of small things, so I start there.

  2. 4:00 pm

    The first look

    I give you ten unphotographed minutes together first, then ten photographed ones. The second set is the one you'll frame.

  3. 5:30 pm

    Ceremony

    One take, no retakes. I move like weather — present, then gone — and keep both lenses loaded because vows don't wait for a film advance.

  4. 7:15 pm

    Golden hour

    Twenty minutes stolen from your own party, usually with dirt on my knees. This is the portrait session that pretends it isn't one.

  5. 10:30 pm

    The send-off

    Sparklers or taillights, shoes in hand, somebody's uncle crying again. I shoot the last frame of film on the last thing that happens — always the right one.

05 / Behind the Lens

Hi — I'm Elena.

I've photographed weddings for eleven years, starting with a borrowed Mamiya and an unreasonable belief that the best pictures happen between the posed ones. That belief has survived four hundred weddings, three continents of jet lag, and one wedding officiant who insisted on karaoke.

I shoot film for the feeling and digital for the certainty — usually both at once. My couples tend to be people who hate having photos taken, which is fortunate, because I'm mostly interested in the moments when they forget I'm there.
