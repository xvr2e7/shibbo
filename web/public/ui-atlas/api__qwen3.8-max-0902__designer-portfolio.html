02 — Process

Straightforward, by design.

Five steps, no theater. The order changes; the discipline doesn’t. The point of all of it is getting something real in front of real people as early as you can stand.

Capability deck (PDF) ↗
  1. S.01

    Discover

    Interviews, diary studies, merciless questions. The goal isn’t a deck — it’s a shared map of what’s actually going on, including the politics.

    • Research repo
    • Problem brief
    • Stakeholder map
  2. S.02

    Define

    Pick one wedge. Write the success metric before drawing a single screen. This is where I’ll politely argue for doing less, better.

    • North-star flow
    • Success criteria
    • Scope trade-offs
  3. S.03

    Prototype

    Fidelity follows the question. Clickable within days, tested with real people weekly — opinions are cheap and behavior is honest.

    • Hi-fi prototype
    • Motion spec
    • Test plan
  4. S.04

    Validate

    Watch five people try to use the thing. Rewrite it. Repeat until the awkward stops. Every round gets logged so decisions stay legible.

    • Usability findings
    • Iteration log
    • Kill list
  5. S.05

    Ship & measure

    I stay through engineering handoff, QA, and the first metrics read. A design isn’t finished when it’s approved — it’s finished when the numbers say so.

    • Annotated specs
    • Baseline dashboard
    • Retro notes

03 — Beliefs

Four things I won’t compromise on.

Formed in shipping, revised occasionally, defended often.

  • 01

    Clarity is a feature.

    The best compliment for an interface is that nobody ever talks about it. If it needs a tour, it isn’t done.

  • 02

    Ship to learn.

    A prototype in a user’s hands beats a perfect deck in a shared drive. Momentum is a design material too.

  • 03

    Systems outlive screens.

    I design the rule, not just the instance — so the tenth screen is better than the first, and the next designer thanks me.

  • 04

    Taste is a decision, not a mood.

    Every pixel should be able to explain itself. “It felt right” is a fine instinct and a terrible rationale.

04 — About

Nine years at the seam of research, engineering & taste.

I’m June — a product designer based in Oslo. Since 2016 I’ve taken products from first sketch to shipped software for health systems, payment companies, and stubborn early-stage teams. My sweet spot is the unglamorous middle: the part where something complex and political has to become something obvious.

I trained as an industrial designer, which explains the habits — constraints first, ergonomics always, honest materials even when they’re pixels. I keep a shelf of failed prototypes beside my desk. It keeps me honest.
