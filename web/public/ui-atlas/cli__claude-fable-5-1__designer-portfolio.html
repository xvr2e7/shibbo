Copenhagen, 2026 Maren

I care about the version of a product that still works on a bad day.

Most interfaces are designed for the happy path. I spend most of my time on the other ones: the tired user, the slow connection, the form filled out at 11pm with a kid on one hip. Getting those right tends to make the rest better too.

I work best embedded with small, senior teams where design, engineering, and product sit at the same table. I write a lot, prototype in code when it helps, and I'd rather ship something modest and learn than present something perfect and wait.

How I work

Three habits, not a framework
01

Find the real question

Briefs usually describe a solution. I spend the first weeks talking to users and support teams until the underlying problem is boring and obvious.

  • Stakeholder interviews
  • Support ticket mining
  • Problem framing doc
02

Prototype to disagree

Instead of one polished direction, I bring two or three rough ones that make different bets. Disagreement early is cheap. Disagreement after launch is not.

  • Divergent concepts
  • Coded prototypes
  • Weekly usability sessions
03

Ship, measure, trim

I stay through launch and the quarter after it. Half of good design is removing what didn't earn its place once real behaviour shows up.

  • Instrumentation plan
  • Post-launch review
  • Design system contributions
