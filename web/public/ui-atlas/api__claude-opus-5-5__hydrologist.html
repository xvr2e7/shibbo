About

I'm a hydrologist who has spent twenty years standing in cold water holding instruments, and the last eight trying to write down what it felt like.

My research focuses on how rivers move sediment and how floodplains store water between storms. I've worked on gauging networks in the American West, delta restoration in Louisiana, and river ice monitoring in interior Alaska. Most of what I've learned came from technicians, farmers, and fishing guides who had watched one stretch of water for longer than any dataset.

The essays here sit between science and memoir. They try to be accurate without being dry, and to stay honest about uncertainty, which is the most important thing a hydrologist can tell you.
