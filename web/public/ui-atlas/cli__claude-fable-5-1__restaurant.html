Our Story

A house, not a restaurant

"Beit" means home, and that was always the idea. When Khalil and Nour left the village of Douma for a new country, they brought one suitcase of clothes and one of spices. They opened a six-table café with Nour's recipes and Khalil's stubborn insistence that nothing leaves the kitchen he wouldn't serve his own mother.

Today their daughter Rima runs the floor, their son Sami runs the grill, and the grandchildren argue about who gets to roll the vine leaves. Nour still comes in on Sundays to make the kibbeh herself. She says nobody else gets the onion right.

Feed people like you'll see them again tomorrow. Because you will. — Nour Khalil, matriarch

Slow food

Stews start at dawn. Chickpeas soak overnight. Nothing is rushed.

Honest sourcing

Lamb from one farm we've known for twenty years. Olive oil from cousins in the Chouf.

Open table

Big families, solo diners, kids under the table. Everyone belongs here.

The Menu

Made to be shared

Order a few mezze for the table, then a grill or a stew each. Or just ask Rima and let her feed you.

Allergies or dietary needs? Most mezze are naturally vegan, and we'll happily adapt dishes. Tell us when you book or when you sit down.
