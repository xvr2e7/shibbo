Why urban trees

A tree is the cheapest piece of public infrastructure a city can build.

In the neighborhoods we serve, tree canopy can differ by 30 percentage points from one side of a highway to the other — and so can summer temperatures, asthma rates, and electricity bills.

Cooler streets

Mature street trees drop surface temperatures by up to 25°F and air temperatures by 11°F — real protection during heat waves that hit low-canopy blocks hardest.

Cleaner air

Our urban forest filters an estimated 740 tons of pollutants a year, easing the particulate load along truck corridors and school routes.

Less flooding

Each mature canopy intercepts thousands of gallons of stormwater annually, taking pressure off aging sewers and flood-prone basements.

Stronger blocks

Tree-lined streets see more foot traffic, more neighbors who know each other, and measurable drops in reported crime and stress.

Where we work

We plant where the canopy gap is widest.

Canopy isn't distributed by accident. We use city heat maps, asthma data, and a century of redlining records to rank every block — then we knock on doors and let residents decide what gets planted, and where.

Our goal: 30% canopy coverage in every priority neighborhood by 2035, with no block left under 15%.

How it works

Planting is the easy part. Keeping trees alive is the work.

A tree needs 20 gallons a week for its first three summers. That's why every planting starts with a neighbor who says yes — and ends with a care plan, not a photo op.

Listen

Block captains and community meetings tell us where shade is missing and what species neighbors want on their sidewalk.

Design

Our arborists match species to soil, sightlines, and power lines, then cut the pits and amend compacted city dirt.

Plant

Saturday crews of 40–120 volunteers put trees in the ground, mulch, stake, and water them in before noon.

Tend

Three years of watering, pruning, and check-ins by paid youth crews and resident stewards. 92% make it.

Our programs

Four ways we grow the urban forest.

🌳 Street Tree Stewardship

Free trees, pits, and three years of care for any resident on a priority block. Sign up and we handle the city permits.

28,400 trees to date

🏫 Schoolyard Forests

Turning asphalt playgrounds into mini-forests with 61 partner schools — shade for recess, outdoor classrooms, and living curriculum.

61 schools · 9,100 students

🔥 Heat Island Response

Rapid canopy deployment on the hottest measured blocks, paired with shade structures and cooling-center wayfinding.

14 districts mapped

🌱 Youth Green Jobs

Paid apprenticeships for 16–24 year olds in arboriculture, with ISA certification, tools, and job placement at the end.

212 graduates · 84% placed
