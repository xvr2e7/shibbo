The method

Thirty-six hours, then four.

Every loaf is flour, water, salt and time. That is why there’s no second batch: the bread you buy on Saturday was started on Thursday night.

  1. Day one · 20:00

    Feed Maud

    Our starter has been going since 2017. She gets fresh flour and a warm spot by the oven.

  2. Day two · 08:00

    Mix

    Stoneground flour from a mill forty miles away, water, sea salt. Nothing else goes in.

  3. Day two · 09:00–14:00

    Fold and wait

    Four folds by hand, an hour apart. The dough tells us when it’s ready, not the clock.

  4. Day two · 15:00

    Shape, then cold

    Into baskets and into the cold room for sixteen hours. This is where the flavour comes from.

  5. Day three · 03:30

    Oven on

    Six loads, baked darker than you might expect. The crust should crackle as it cools.

  6. Day three · 07:30

    Hatch up

    Still warm. There’s usually a queue, and it moves quickly.

  7. Day three · ~12:00

    Gone.

    Shelf empty, door locked, and the next mix is already under way.
