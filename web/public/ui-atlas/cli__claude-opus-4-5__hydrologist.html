About

I study rivers—how they move, what they carry, where they go when we're not watching. For twenty years I've measured streamflow, mapped floodplains, and testified at more public hearings than I care to count.

These essays are my attempt to translate what the data says into something that might matter to someone who's never held a current meter. Rivers are not pipes. They are not problems to be solved. They are processes, older than our cities and more patient than our plans.

I live in the watershed of the Willamette, where it rains enough to remind you that water is the default and dry ground is the exception.
