01

The river remembers what the maps forget

On old channels, new boundaries, and the stories a landscape keeps.

 Rivers & memory +

A map draws a river as a line. Walk beside one long enough and the line begins to loosen: a damp hollow beyond the bank, a stand of willows where the channel used to run, a bend slowly making its way across a field. The river is still writing, even where we have stopped looking.

02

On the quiet work of a floodplain

Why making room for water is also making room for life.

 Floodplains +

When a river spills over its banks, it does not leave its course so much as return to an older, wider version of it. Across the floodplain, water slows. Sediment settles. Seeds travel. What looks, from a distance, like disorder is a landscape doing its patient work.

03

Where the water goes in August

A summer walk through the invisible half of a watershed.

 Watersheds +

By late summer, the creek behind my house is narrow enough to step across. It is tempting to think of the missing water as gone. But beneath the gravel, through roots and porous stone, another current continues: slower, cooler, and no less a part of the river.

04

A field guide to paying attention

What a notebook, a pair of boots, and an hour beside a stream can teach us.

 Field notes +

Begin with what is ordinary. The height of a water stain on a stone. The place where foam collects. The sound a shallow reach makes before rain, and the different sound it makes after. Attention is not a gift some people have; it is a practice, built one observation at a time.

05

The shape of a watershed

Following a single raindrop into a much larger story.

 Water & place +

A watershed is a simple idea with far-reaching consequences: every place where rain falls and finds its way to the same outlet. It connects a rooftop to a tributary, a hillside to a harbor, and each of us to places we may never see.
