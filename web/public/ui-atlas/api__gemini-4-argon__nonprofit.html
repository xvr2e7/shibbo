Botanical Field Guide

Climate-adapted native stock.

We never plant invasive monocultures. Every sapling is grown in gravel beds for dense fibrous root architecture and selected for drought resilience, utility-line clearance, and deep taproots that won't heave sidewalks.

ZONES 3–9

Swamp White Oak

Quercus bicolor

An urban powerhouse tolerant of compacted road-salt soils and alternating flooded bioswales. Supports over 530 native caterpillar species.

CO₂ / Yr (Mature) 58.4 lbs
Canopy Spread 50 – 60 ft
Stormwater / Yr 1,420 gal
Salt Tolerance High
ZONES 3–8

Kentucky Coffeetree

Gymnocladus dioicus

Thrives in punishing heat islands and alkaline concrete rubble. Deep taproots grow straight down, keeping historic stone sidewalks level.

CO₂ / Yr (Mature) 49.2 lbs
Canopy Spread 40 – 50 ft
Stormwater / Yr 1,180 gal
Drought Rating Extreme
ZONES 4–10

Bald Cypress

Taxodium distichum

Our premier curbside bioswale tree. Absorbs flash-flood street runoff while feathery needles trap fine diesel exhaust particulates.

CO₂ / Yr (Mature) 54.0 lbs
Canopy Spread 25 – 35 ft
Stormwater / Yr 1,890 gal
Wind Resistance Superior
ZONES 4–9

Allegheny Serviceberry

Amelanchier laevis

Compact stature engineered for narrow streets under overhead utility wires. Early spring nectar source for native urban bees and songbirds.

CO₂ / Yr (Mature) 26.8 lbs
Canopy Spread 15 – 25 ft
Wire Safe Yes (<25 ft)
Wildlife Value 38 Bird Spp.
