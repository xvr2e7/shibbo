٠١ 

Everything arrives at once, because that's how love works.

The sofra isn't a menu — it's the table itself, covered all at once and kept refilling. Start with mezze and argue about them. Then the charcoal. Then coffee. Nothing is a course; everything is an invitation.

"A guest is a gift from God. The rest is just logistics." — Wadad, most mornings
1968 — Beirut

A wedding at Hamra

Wadad marries Samir Karam. Her dowry, she'll remind you, was a copper saj and strong opinions about tabbouleh.

1974 — Ashrafieh

The counter opens

Samir starts a tiny saj stall. Bread sells out daily by three; Wadad keeps adding plates until the mezze count reaches seven. ٧ أطباق

1982 — The leaving

Two suitcases

The family leaves mid-war with clothes, photographs, and one sealed jar of baharat. The recipes travel inside a prayer book, folded twice.

1991 — First dining room

Nine tables, one oven

Beit el-Karam opens its doors here — handwritten menu, Wadad at the stove, Samir at the door, children washing glasses after school.

2003 — Second generation

The kids take over

Rabih claims the charcoal; his wife Layla takes the oven. The tabbouleh arguments continue, uninterrupted and necessary.

Today

Same saj, same spice, same open door

Nour — born the year the restaurant opened — runs the sweet corner. The maamoul moulds are Teta's. So is the rule: nobody leaves hungry.

٠٣ 

Three things we refuse to rush.

١

Bread on the hour

The saj dome never cools from noon till close. A manoushe turns in forty seconds flat — miss one and you wait for the next. Nobody has ever minded.

Every hour · from noon
٢

Arak poured from a height

One-third arak, two-thirds cold water, ice last — always last. It clouds over like morning mist and tastes like the mountains it comes from.

Arak hour · Tue–Thu, 5–6
٣

Sunday kibbeh

The whole family rolls kibbeh every Sunday at eleven. Extra hands are welcome, corrections are free, and so far there have been zero injuries.

Sundays · 11:00 · all welcome
٤

Coffee, read afterwards

Cardamom-heavy and poured from a height. When the cup is empty we'll turn it over and read your fortune. Ours is always that you'll come back.

Fortunes · free of charge
