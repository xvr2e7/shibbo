Tally

2026 · Fintech

A banking app for freelancers that quietly sets tax aside, so "what can I actually spend?" has one honest answer.

iOS & Android0 → 1 +41% week-4 retention
The short version
Problem

Freelancers saw one big balance and spent it. Tax season arrived as a shock, and churn followed two months later.

Approach

Interviewed 26 self-employed people, then rebuilt the home screen around a single "safe to spend" figure, with automatic set-asides shown as a calm progress bar rather than an alarm.

Outcome

Week-4 retention rose 41%, and 72% of new accounts turned on set-asides during onboarding.

Fieldnote

2025 · Health ops

Scheduling for small clinics. A calendar that spots its own gaps and fills them from the waitlist before the front desk notices.

Web appB2B −32% empty slots
The short version
Problem

Receptionists juggled a calendar, a paper waitlist and a phone. Cancellations became empty chairs.

Approach

Shadowed five front desks for a week each. Designed a one-tap "offer this slot" flow and reworked the calendar's density so a full day fits on a 13-inch screen.

Outcome

Unfilled appointments fell by 32% across 140 clinics, and booking a patient dropped from eleven clicks to four.

Orbit Design System

2024 · Platform

One component library for six product teams and three platforms, built so the easy path and the accessible path are the same path.

Design systemTokens 3× faster to ship UI
The short version
Problem

Fourteen button variants, four date pickers and no shared language between design and engineering.

Approach

Audited every screen in production, cut the inventory to 48 components, and paired with engineers on a token pipeline that syncs Figma to code nightly.

Outcome

Adopted by all six teams within two quarters. New feature UI ships roughly three times faster, and accessibility bugs dropped by more than half.

Kestrel

2022 · Wearables

Companion app for a sleep ring. Fewer charts, one suggestion a day, and a morning summary you can read before coffee.

iOSData viz 4.8★ App Store rating
The short version
Problem

The first version showed eleven metrics every morning. People felt graded, not helped, and stopped opening it.

Approach

Ran a four-week diary study, then cut the morning view to one ring, one number and a single suggestion written in plain language.

Outcome

Daily opens rose 58%, and the rating climbed from 3.9 to 4.8 within three releases.

How I work

Three habits
  1. 01

    Sit with the people

    I'd rather spend a week at a front desk than a month in a workshop. The best brief is watching someone work around your product.

  2. 02

    Prototype the hard part

    Happy paths are easy to draw. I build the messy state first (the error, the empty screen, the edge case) because that's where trust is won.

  3. 03

    Stay until it ships

    Handoff isn't the finish line. I pair with engineers through QA, and I measure what happens after launch.
