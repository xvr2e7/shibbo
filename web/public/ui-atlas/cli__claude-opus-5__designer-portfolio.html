Selected work

Four of eleven case studies
01Lumen Bank·2025

Onboarding for two million first-time savers

A neobank was losing most of its sign-ups before the first deposit. I rebuilt identity verification as a conversation instead of a form — progressive disclosure, honest waiting states, and a recovery path for every dead end.

Lead Product DesignerResearchFintech / KYCiOS · Android · Web
Drop-off down 41% · support tickets −28%

Context

Sign-up completion sat at 38%. Compliance required nine documents; the product asked for all of them on screen three, with no explanation of why.

What I did

Ran 22 sessions with people who had abandoned the flow. Resequenced verification around trust moments, designed state-by-state error recovery, and co-wrote every line of copy with legal.

Outcome

  • 41% fewer abandonments at verification
  • +310k funded accounts in two quarters
  • −28% onboarding support contacts
  • Flow adopted as the pattern for three other markets
02Verdant Health·2024

A care plan people actually follow

Clinicians wrote excellent plans that patients ignored. We stopped designing a tracker and designed a daily companion — one honest question a day, tuned to how someone was really doing, with clinician handoff built in.

Design LeadBehavioural researchDigital health0 → 1
Weekly retention 2.3× · plan adherence +34%

Context

A 0→1 companion app for people managing type 2 diabetes. Existing tools demanded daily logging that 80% of users dropped within eleven days.

What I did

Diary study with 14 patients and 6 nurses. Reframed the product around minimum viable input, designed the escalation rules with the clinical team, and shipped a four-week pilot before any visual polish.

Outcome

  • 2.3× week-8 retention vs. the legacy tracker
  • +34% self-reported plan adherence
  • Nurse review time cut from 18 to 6 minutes per patient
  • Now in two NHS trust pilots
03Keystone·2023 — 2024

The design system as a shared argument

Four product teams, four interpretations of a button. Keystone wasn’t a component library so much as a set of agreements — tokens, accessibility baked in at the primitive level, and a contribution path that engineers actually used.

Systems DesignDesign opsWCAG 2.2 AAFigma + code parity
New screens ship 60% faster

Context

A logistics SaaS with 41 one-off button variants, three competing date pickers and an accessibility backlog nobody owned.

What I did

Audited 600 screens, defined a three-tier token model, paired with two engineers to ship primitives first, and ran fortnightly open critiques so the system stayed everyone’s.

Outcome

  • New screens from brief to build 60% faster
  • 94% component adoption across four teams
  • Accessibility defects down 71%
  • Documented contribution model, still running without me
04Northbound Atlas·2022

Dispatch for people with eleven tabs open

Freight coordinators were running €40m of cargo out of spreadsheets and phone calls. Atlas gave them one surface built around exceptions — the dashboard only speaks up when something is going wrong.

Senior Product DesignerContextual inquiryComplex tablesOps tooling
Exception handling 3.5× faster

Context

Nine coordinators, 1,400 weekly shipments, zero shared source of truth. Delays were discovered by customers before the operator knew.

What I did

Three days shadowing night dispatch in Rotterdam. Designed an exception-first information hierarchy, a keyboard-complete table, and alert thresholds the coordinators tuned themselves.

Outcome

  • Time to resolve an exception 3.5× faster
  • −62% late-delivery penalties year on year
  • Spreadsheet workflow retired in 7 weeks
  • SUS score up from 54 to 86

How I work

Three commitments
01

Go where the work happens

The best insight is rarely in the analytics. It’s in a warehouse at 2am, or in the workaround someone built in a spreadsheet. I start by watching, then earn the right to have an opinion.

02

Make the argument visible

A prototype settles debates that a document prolongs. I build the smallest honest artefact that lets a team disagree productively — and I’ll happily throw mine away when the evidence says so.

03

Ship, then stay

Design isn’t finished at handoff. I sit with engineering through build, watch the first week of real usage, and treat the follow-up release as part of the original commitment.

About

Background & capabilities
Mira AdeyemiLagos → London

I’m a product designer who likes the hard, unphotogenic parts of software — permissions, edge cases, the fourteenth state of an empty table. Those are usually where the real experience lives.

I started in industrial design in Lagos, which taught me that a product is whatever survives contact with a real user in a real room. I’ve since spent twelve years in digital: five as the first designer at early-stage companies, seven leading small teams inside larger ones. I’m most useful where the domain is complicated, the stakes are genuine, and nobody can quite agree on what the problem is yet.

Outside work: long-distance swimming, letterpress, and a slowly growing collection of badly designed appliance manuals.
