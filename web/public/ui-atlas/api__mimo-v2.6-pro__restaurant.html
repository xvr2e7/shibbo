Our Story

A kitchen that started
on a courtyard floor.

In 1978 our grandmother Halima left Tripoli with a tin of olive oil, a copper saj and a notebook of recipes written in pencil. She began feeding neighbours from a two-room flat above a greengrocer. People kept coming back.

Today her grandchildren run the same kitchen — bigger room, same notebook. The hummus is still whipped by hand, the meat still arrives whole and is butchered here, and the bread still leaves the oven every nine minutes. We buy from four growers we've known for decades, and we say no to anything that comes in a tin.

“Food should taste of the land it came from — and of the hands that made it.”
Layla Haddad
Head Chef · third generation

The Sunday Table

One table.
Everything we know.
Served at once.

Every Sunday we lay out the full spread for the table — the way a Lebanese family actually eats. No courses, no waiting. Just a table that fills up and stays full, and bread arriving until someone surrenders.

  • 1 Eight cold mezze — hummus, moutabal, muhammara, fattoush, tabbouleh, warak enab, labneh and pickles.
  • 2 Four from the charcoal — kofta, shish taouk, lamb chops and grilled vegetables, carved at the table.
  • 3 Sweets and coffee — knefeh to share, ma'amoul and Turkish coffee, poured slowly.
  • 4 Unlimited saj bread and our own olive oil for dipping. This part is not negotiable.

How We Cook

Four rules we
never break.

It would be easier to buy the hummus in. It would be faster to use a gas grill. But there's a reason people drive across the city for it — and it's these four things.

01

Bought at dawn

Vegetables come from the market before sunrise. Whatever looks best that morning decides part of the menu.

02

Ground here

Za'atar, seven-spice and baharat are blended in-house each week in a stone mill we've had for thirty years.

03

Over real coals

No gas, no shortcuts. The charcoal grill runs from noon, and the smoke is the flavour you can't fake.

04

Bread every 9 min

Saj bread is rolled and blistered to order. If it sits longer than ten minutes, it doesn't leave the kitchen.
