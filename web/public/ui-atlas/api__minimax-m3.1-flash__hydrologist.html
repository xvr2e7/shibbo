Bio

Elin Vassallo

I am a hydrologist and writer based in Porto, where I keep a bicycle near the river and a barometer on the sill. I hold a PhD in fluvial geomorphology and spent nine years as a river gauger in northern Spain — reading a staff gauge twice a day, in rain, in fog, and in the particular stillness of an August that everyone else was on holiday.

My work lives where measurement meets attention: the sediment budget of a restored floodplain, the cold-water refuge of an alpine tributary, the slow arithmetic of a karst spring that has been filling the same basin since the last glaciation. I write about the numbers, but I am usually trying to get at the thing the number is standing in for.

“A river is not a line on a map. It is a rate of forgetting.”

I teach occasional courses in hydrology, answer questions for the district’s water company, and keep a small archive of gauge plates — hand-drawn, ink on board, one for every year since 2009.
