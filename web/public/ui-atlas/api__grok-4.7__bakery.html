02 — The oven

The odd loaf is often the best one. We do not trim the ear into a logo.

Odd Loaf started because the block needed bread that tasted like someone had been awake for it. One deck oven. One starter. Her name is Mabel, and she is older than the shop. We mix a small bake — enough for Wren Street and the streets that touch it — and then we stop.

Nothing is held behind the counter. Nothing is baked again after lunch to spare a late arrival. If you get here at 12:05 you will meet a clean counter and a baker who has already sat down.

“I set an alarm for bread. I am not ashamed.” — Priya, two doors down

How not to miss it

Come for breakfast.
There is no second sitting.

01

Between 7 and 9 if the miche is the point.

Saturday moves faster than Tuesday. Good weather moves faster than rain. The bell over the door is not decorative.

02

Have a second choice.

Buns leave early. Seeded rye and focaccia linger. If you only love one thing on the list, treat the clock as part of the recipe.

03

We do not do preorders, holds, or a quiet afternoon loaf.

The queue is the allocation. If supper needs bread, think about it at breakfast. What is left at noon is a story, not a product.

03 — The door

Find the green frame. Flour on the step is a good sign.
