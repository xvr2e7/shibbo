About

Mira Halloran is a hydrologist whose fieldwork and essays both begin in the same place: standing in moving water with a meter, trying to say how much.

Trained in surface-water hydrology and fluvial geomorphology, I spent eleven years on gauging networks and sediment-transport studies before I started writing about them. The essays come out of that work — the discharge measurements taken in February rain, the hearings where a watershed is divided into shares, the slow return of a channel after a dam comes out. I am interested in rivers as physical systems and as the oldest things we argue about.

My writing has appeared in Orion, Emergence Magazine, Places Journal, n+1, and elsewhere, and has been anthologized twice. I am currently at work on a book about the century we spent trying to hold rivers still. I live in the Willamette Valley, within walking distance of a stream that is not on most maps.
