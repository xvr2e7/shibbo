The whole business, really

Her name is Gert,
and she's older than the shop

Gert has been alive since March 2014 — four months before we opened the door. She's woken at 6:40, fed with 100g of flour and 100g of water, and by eight she's lively enough to raise a loaf of bread on her own.

We have never skipped a feed. Not once. Not for holidays, not for the week the flood came up to the step, not even the fortnight when our oven died and we baked in a domestic oven that ran hot.

A day on the bench

Six hours, start to shutter

  • 6:40 am
    Gert gets fed. Flour, water, and one bubble if we're lucky.
  • 7:00 am
    Doors open. The first batch goes in, the kettle goes on.
  • 9:15 am
    Shaping. Thirty-six loaves, one bench, flour everywhere.
  • 11:00 am
    Batch two comes out. This is the good hour. This is the hour.
  • 12:00 pm
    Sold out. We mean it, and we close the shutter behind you.
  • 4:00 pm
    Gert's second feed. Tomorrow starts now, gently.
