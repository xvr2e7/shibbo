Why urban trees

The cheapest infrastructure a city can build.

A mature street tree returns about three dollars in public benefit for every dollar spent on it. Here is where that value shows up.

Beat the heat

Neighborhoods without canopy run up to 15°F hotter on summer afternoons. Shade trees lower surface temperatures, cut cooling bills, and save lives during heat waves.

Breathe easier

Leaves filter fine particulates and ozone from the air at street level, right where kids walk to school. Blocks with trees see measurably fewer asthma visits.

Soak up stormwater

A single large tree can intercept thousands of gallons of rain each year, easing pressure on aging sewers and keeping polluted runoff out of rivers.

Steady neighborhoods

Tree-lined streets slow traffic, raise property values without pricing people out, and give neighbors a reason to be outside and know each other.

Better mental health

People who can see trees from their windows report lower stress and recover faster from illness. Green views are not a luxury; they are a public health tool.

Habitat in the city

Native oaks, maples, and redbuds feed birds, bees, and butterflies that have nowhere else to go. Our planting lists are built around local species.

How it works

From a bare sidewalk to a shady street.

01

Map the gaps

We combine satellite canopy data with heat and health records to find the blocks that need trees most, then knock on doors to ask residents what they actually want.

02

Pick the right tree

Our arborists match each site with a native species that fits the soil, the power lines, and the climate the city will have in 2060, not the one it had in 1990.

03

Plant together

Neighbors, volunteers, and our crews plant side by side on a Saturday morning. Every household gets a tree-care guide and a direct line to our team.

04

Care for three years

We water, mulch, stake, and prune through the critical establishment period, and train paid local Tree Stewards to take over long term.

Programs

Five ways we grow the canopy.

Flagship

Street Trees for All

Our core program plants free street trees in front of homes in low-canopy neighborhoods, with three years of included care. Over 140,000 trees and counting.

Fund a street tree → Jobs

Tree Stewards

A paid, 12-week training that turns residents into certified tree-care professionals, with job placement at city forestry departments and landscapers.

 Schools

Shaded Schoolyards

Replacing asphalt with groves at public schools so recess happens under trees instead of on a 140°F blacktop.

 Water

Rain Gardens

Trees paired with bioswales along flood-prone corridors to catch stormwater before it hits the sewer.

 Policy

Canopy Equity Lab

Open data and advocacy that helps cities write tree ordinances and budgets that put under-canopied neighborhoods first.
