The Record

Made in a grain barn,
kept in the dark

Eight days, one room, a borrowed tape machine, and no click track. Here's how Phosphene happened.

We drove out to Astoria in February with a van full of half-broken amps and a plan to make something small. The barn had no heat, so we tracked the drums first just to stay warm, and the tape kept everything — the footfalls on the floorboards, the wind in the rafters, the dog next door that somehow ends up on track four.

What came back felt less like a set of songs and more like a room we'd all been living in. Phosphene is about the shapes you see when there's nothing to see: afterimages, memory, the hour after everyone leaves. It's the warmest and most honest thing we've made.

“We stopped trying to fix the takes and started listening to what the room was already doing.” — Junia Vale, vocals & guitar

Mixed on a 1974 console by Ada Ferro and mastered half-speed to keep the low end soft and human. The sleeve was shot on 120mm film at 5am on the last morning of tracking, when the fog burned off the river all at once.
