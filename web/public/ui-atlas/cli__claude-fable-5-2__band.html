      We wanted it to sound like a town in the off-season. Everything still there, nobody watching.
      — Maren Okafor, vocals & guitar

      Salt Lantern spent four months in the old terminal building at the end of the harbour road, hauling amps past the ticket windows every morning and packing down before the tide came over the car park. The room did half the arranging: long reverb off the tiled walls, a radiator that hums in B flat, gulls on every quiet take.

      Where their debut was all nervous energy, Low Tide Almanac takes its time. Songs open slowly and stay open. There's pedal steel on three tracks, a borrowed harmonium on two, and one instrumental recorded in a single pass at four in the morning.

      It's a record about a place, but mostly about the people who keep the lights on there.
