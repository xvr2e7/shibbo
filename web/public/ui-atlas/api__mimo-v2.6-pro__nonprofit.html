The case for canopy

Concrete holds the heat. Trees let it go.

On a July afternoon, an unshaded block can run more than seven degrees hotter than a leafy one two streets over. That difference is not evenly shared. In most American cities, the hottest neighborhoods are also the ones with the fewest trees — and the highest rates of asthma, heat illness, and energy poverty.

  • Equity first, always

    87% of our plantings go to neighborhoods below the city's median canopy coverage.

  • Care, not just planting

    Every tree gets three years of watering, pruning, and monitoring. Planting is the easy part.

  • Neighbors lead

    Block captains choose the species, host the planting day, and keep the water going.

Our method

Listen. Plant. Care.

We don't arrive with a plan and a truck. We arrive with questions — then we stay for a decade. Here's what a Rootstock neighborhood partnership actually looks like.

Step one

Listen to the block

We walk every street with residents, mapping shade gaps, heat exposure, and the places kids actually wait for the bus. The community decides where trees go — not us.

01
Step two

Plant with neighbors

We source regionally native, climate-resilient stock from local nurseries and plant on volunteer Saturdays. Every tree is sited for the wires, the pipes, and the next fifty years.

02
Step three

Care for three years

Watering bags, summer irrigation crews, pruning, pest monitoring, and free replacement if a tree fails. Then we hand the stewardship to a trained local block captain.

03

What we run

Three programs, one long view.

Each one is designed to outlast us — built around local ownership, local jobs, and species that will still be standing in 2075.

Street trees

The Street Tree Corps

Our year-round crew plants and maintains sidewalk trees on heat-vulnerable blocks, and trains neighborhood hires as urban forestry apprentices along the way.

School Forests

We turn asphalt schoolyards into working outdoor classrooms — native groves, rain gardens, and a curriculum that puts students in charge of the data.
