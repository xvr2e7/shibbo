قصتنا Our story

It began with one woman, one copper pot, and six tables.

In 1962, our grandmother Mary — everyone called her Teta Mary — traded her village kitchen for a tiny storefront on Cedar Lane. She cooked whatever the morning market gave her, from a notebook of recipes she carried in her suitcase, stained before it ever reached us.

Three generations later, not much has changed. The notebook still sits on the pass. We still pound hummus in the same stone mortar, still shape man'oushe in the wood oven at dawn, and still send you home with extra fattoush "for the road."
