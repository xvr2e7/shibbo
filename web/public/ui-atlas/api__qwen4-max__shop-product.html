The Craft

Clay remembers heat.
So does your coffee.

Every Meridian begins as a lump of iron-flecked stoneware on the wheel. Our potters throw the cone in a single pull, carve the spiral ribs by hand while the clay is leather-hard, then fire it to 1280°C — hot enough that the body vitrifies and holds brewing heat far longer than porcelain or plastic ever could.

Why does that matter? Temperature stability is the quiet variable in every pour-over. A cold dripper steals heat from your slurry in the first thirty seconds, right when extraction matters most. Thick stoneware walls, pre-warmed with a quick rinse, keep the bed within a degree or two of target from bloom to final drip.

The spiral ribs aren't decoration. They lift the filter off the wall so water flows down and out — never around the grounds — giving you a clean, sweet cup with none of the bypass bitterness of flat-walled cones.

Brew Guide

Four minutes to a
remarkably good cup.

Our house recipe for the Meridian, dialed for a medium roast. Slide the dial to scale it to your morning.

01

Rinse & preheat

Seat a #02 filter, pour hot water through it into your mug. This washes the paper and — crucially — warms the stoneware. Dump the rinse water.

02

Bloom, 0:00–0:45

Add medium-fine grounds. Pour twice the coffee weight in water at 94°C, swirl gently, and wait 45 seconds while the bed degasses.

03

Pour in circles, 0:45–2:00

Add the remaining water in two slow, steady spirals, keeping the slurry level even. Avoid pouring on the paper itself.

04

Drawdown, by ~3:30

A gentle swirl at the end flattens the bed. Total brew should finish between 3:00 and 3:45. Grind finer if fast, coarser if slow.

Questions

Before you pour.

Will it fit my mug or carafe?+
The base ring is 10 cm wide with a stepped collar, so it seats securely on anything with a 6–11 cm opening — standard mugs, most 300–600 ml glass servers, and wide-mouth carafes. If it wobbles on your vessel, returns are free for 30 days.
Which filters does it take?+
Any standard #02 conical paper filter — Hario, Cafec, generic store brands, bleached or unbleached. We recommend rinsing natural filters a little longer to keep papery notes out of the cup.
Is the glaze food-safe? Can it go in the dishwasher?+
Yes and yes. All four glazes are lead- and cadmium-free, fired to full vitrification at 1280°C, and tested for thermal shock. Dishwasher, microwave, and daily use are all fine. Avoid direct stovetop flame and sudden freezer-to-boiling swings.
Why does each one look slightly different?+
Because a person made it, not a mold. Throwing marks, rib spacing, and glaze pooling vary piece to piece — kiln atmosphere shifts color a shade or two between shelves. We consider that the point. Dimensions stay within ±2 mm so brewing behavior is identical.
