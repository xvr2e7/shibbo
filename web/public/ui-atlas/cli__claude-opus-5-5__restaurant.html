Our Story

It started with a single pot of lentil soup.

When Nadia Haddad arrived with her husband Elias in 1996, she missed one thing above all: the noise of a full table. So she cooked — for neighbors, for church, for anyone who knocked. Two years later the neighbors convinced her to open a door of her own.

Today her son Karim runs the grill, his wife Rana bakes every loaf of bread in the stone oven, and their daughter Leila keeps the dining room humming. Sitto still comes in every morning to taste the toum. If it's not right, it doesn't go out.

No shortcuts, no freezers full of shortcuts — just olive oil, lemons, patience, and the same recipes written in Arabic in a notebook that never leaves the kitchen.

“Food made with love tastes different. You don't need to believe me — just eat.” — Sitto Nadia, founder
