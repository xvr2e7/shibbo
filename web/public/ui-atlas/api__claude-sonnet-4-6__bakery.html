Our story

Baked by one person
in a tiny kitchen

Odd Loaf started in 2019 when Mira began milling her own flour and baking for neighbours on her street. What began as a weekend hobby grew into a small but fiercely loved bakery operating out of a converted corner shop.

Every loaf is hand-shaped, cold-proofed overnight, and baked in a stone deck oven. We use organic grain from a single farm sixty miles north, freshly milled each week. Nothing frozen. Nothing rushed.

The name comes from the first loaf Mira ever sold — it had a lopsided ear and an almost-burnt bottom. The neighbour who bought it called it her favourite bread in the city.

How we bake

Slow bread for
impatient mornings

01
Mill the grain
Organic wheat arrives whole. We mill it fresh each week on a stone burr mill to preserve flavour and nutrition that roller-milled flour loses.
02
Build the leaven
Our starter — now six years old — is fed 12 hours before mixing. It drives the entire fermentation without a single grain of commercial yeast.
03
Long cold proof
Shaped loaves rest in the fridge for up to 48 hours. This is where the flavour develops — slowly, unhurriedly, the way good things do.
04
Bake at dawn
Into a 500°C stone deck oven with steam for the first few minutes. Each loaf is scored by hand, one at a time, before the sun comes up.
