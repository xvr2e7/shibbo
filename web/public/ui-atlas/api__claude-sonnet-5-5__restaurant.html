Our story

A kitchen table that grew into a restaurant

In 1989, Elias and Layla Haddad left their village in the mountains above Batroun with two suitcases, a copper pot and a handwritten notebook of recipes. They opened Beit Haddad — "the Haddad house" — with six tables and a promise: nobody would ever leave hungry.

Today their children, Nadia and Karim, run the floor and the grill, and their granddaughter Maya is learning to roll grape leaves at Sitto's elbow. We still make our labneh weekly, grind our own spice blends, and bake bread every hour we're open.

When you eat here, you're a guest in our home. Expect second helpings, a free cup of cardamom coffee, and probably a hug.

— The Haddad family
