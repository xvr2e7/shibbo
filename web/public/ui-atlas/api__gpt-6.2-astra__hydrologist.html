01The river remembers its bendsReading the faint handwriting of an old channel.October 2025→

From the road it looks like a bad patch of hay. Walk down into it and the ground gives a little under your heel. The rushes make a crescent so complete that you can stand at one tip and see the other, across a field the river hasn’t crossed in living memory.

On my survey sheet the river is a line. Here it is also a difference in soil, a place where the mower bogs down, a curve the farmer follows without ever needing to draw it.

02A small argument with a gaugeWhat a water level tells us, and what it leaves out.August 2025→

The gauge said the creek was higher than yesterday. The stones I had used as stepping-stones were still dry. I checked my watch, my notebook, and finally the little raft of sticks lodged against the culvert downstream.

Both observations were good. The sticks had backed water up to the gauge but not as far as my crossing. It was the word “creek” in my notebook that needed correcting. I crossed it out and wrote “at the bridge.”

03Where the gravel restsAn afternoon on the inside of a bend.May 2025→

I ate lunch on a bar of flat gray pebbles. Most tilted the same way. With the heel of my hand I could cover six or seven, each one propped against the next like plates left to dry.

The creek beside me was too gentle to shift even one. It takes a little imagination to sit in such a quiet spot and recognize the work of a much louder day.

04The tributary under the streetFollowing a buried stream through town.February 2025→

On the oldest map in the library a blue thread runs straight through the bakery. I took a copy outside. Behind the delivery door the alley pitched steeply down, then up again to meet the next street.

At the far end of town water spilled from a pipe into daylight. Between the map and that mouth I could only guess the route. I marked it in pencil, leaving a gap where I stopped for bread.
