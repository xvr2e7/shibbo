Our Family

Two suitcases, a copper rakwe,
and one wooden mould.

Samira and Georges Haddad left the Bekaa Valley in 1987 with very little — and a stubborn belief that nobody should eat alone.

They opened twelve tables on Mulberry Street and cooked what they knew: hummus whipped by hand, vine leaves rolled before sunrise, lamb over coals until the fat whispered. Thirty-nine years later the tables are the same tables. Georges still lights the grill at four. Samira — Teta to everyone now — comes in on Thursdays to taste the tabbouleh and tell her children they have used too much bourghul.

Today their daughter Nadia runs the kitchen, their son Nabil runs the room and pours the arak, and a third generation does its homework at table nine. Nothing is frozen, nothing is rushed, and nobody leaves without being asked twice if they want more bread.

“Eat, habibi. There is more.”Teta Samira Haddad
