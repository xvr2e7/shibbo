Today's board

What's actually on the shelf.

Counted by hand, updated as the morning goes. If something says gone, it's gone — we don't keep a secret loaf behind the counter. (We keep one. It's for Margaret.)

Counts refresh while you're here. Cash and card both fine; bring a bag if you can. Bread is sliced only on request, and only until the slicer gets temperamental, which is daily.

Why so little bread?

Because we only have the one oven, and it only has the one morning.

A deck oven fits thirty-odd loaves at a time. The starter decides when it's ready, not the schedule. We could bake twice and sell more — we'd also sell worse bread at four in the afternoon, and we'd rather not.

Getting bread

Three ways to not miss out.

01

Come early

Doors at 7:00 sharp. The queue starts around 6:50 and is friendlier than it looks — someone always brings a flask. Everything is on the shelf from the first minute; nothing is held back.

Tue–Sun · 7:00

02

Read the Sunday note

One email a week: what we're baking, which days the rye is on, and an honest warning when a festival or a wedding order is about to eat half the bake.

Sundays · 18:00

03

Reserve for Saturday

Twelve Saturday loaves are set aside for reservations. They open on Thursday at 9:00, go in about four minutes, and must be collected by 10:00 — after that we sell them.

Thursdays · 9:00

Find us

Half a shop, one window, excellent smell.

We're the narrow unit beside the launderette. No seats, no wifi, no table service — but there's a bench outside and the bakery next door to the dry cleaner's will lend you a knife if you ask nicely.
