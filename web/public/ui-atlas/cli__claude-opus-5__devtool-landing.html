Find the query that's eating your p99.

Tern reads your database's own statistics tables, ranks every statement by the time it actually burned, and hands you the index that fixes it. One binary. No agent, no sampling, no dashboard to log into.

What it does

Your database already knows. Tern just asks.

Every mature database keeps precise timing for every statement it has executed. Tern reads those tables directly — no proxy in the hot path, no sampling error, no mystery traces.

Reads the source of truth

pg_stat_statements, MySQL's performance_schema, SQLite's query log. Exact totals, not a 1% sample you have to squint at.

Normalized fingerprints

1.2 million calls with different literals collapse into one ranked statement, so the real cost finally shows up where you can see it.

Plans, annotated

Runs EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) and flags what matters: seq scans, estimates off by 1000×, sorts spilling to disk, lossy bitmap heaps.

Fixes you can paste

Index suggestions come with the column order, the CONCURRENTLY keyword, the disk cost, and an honest before/after estimate.

Safe by construction

A read-only role is all it needs. Tern never reads your table data, and --dry-run is the default for anything that writes.

Fails the build, not your users

tern ci diffs plans against your baseline branch and exits non-zero when a new query blows its latency budget.

How it works

Three commands, start to fixed.

No instrumentation to add, no SDK to import, nothing to deploy. Point it at a connection string.

01

Connect

Any URL your client accepts. A read-only role with pg_monitor is enough.

$ tern init
  ✓ wrote tern.toml
  ✓ pg_stat_statements: ready
02

Rank

Sorted by total time burned — the only ranking that maps to your bill and your p99.

$ tern scan
  2,418 statements
  2 critical, 1 warning
03

Fix

Get the plan, the diagnosis, and the exact DDL. Then keep it fixed in CI.

$ tern explain 1
  12.4ms → 0.31ms
  saves 4h 02m / day

Why another tool

You don't have an observability problem.
You have one bad query.

Most of the time you don't need distributed tracing and a retention plan. You need to know which statement is burning the database, and what to do about it, before lunch.

The usual route

  • Deploy an agent, wait for data to accumulate
  • Learn a query language to ask "what's slow"
  • Sampled traces that miss the 2am spike entirely
  • A dashboard that shows latency but never the plan
  • Per-host pricing that scales with your problem
  • Findings that live in a tab nobody reopens

With Tern

  • One binary, one connection string, answers in 1.2s
  • Ranked list in your terminal, highest cost first
  • Exact totals from the database's own counters
  • The annotated plan and the index, side by side
  • MIT licensed, runs on your laptop or in CI
  • Output is diffable text — commit it, review it
