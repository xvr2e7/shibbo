Why we're always out by noon

One oven holds 24 loaves. We fire it six times before the doors open, and that's the whole day's bread. Here's how it goes.

  1. 2:00

    Wake the dough

    Yesterday's shaped loaves come out of a 36-hour cold retard.

  2. 4:00

    First bake

    Deck oven at 250°C. Steam, score, wait, listen for the crackle.

  3. 7:00

    Doors open

    Usually a queue. Usually someone's dog. Coffee's on the counter.

  4. 10:00

    Racks thin

    Rye and the specials go first. Country loaf hangs on a little longer.

  5. 12:00

    Sold out

    Sign flips. We feed the starter, mix tomorrow, and go home.

Meet the real head baker

Every loaf starts with Gerald.

Gerald is our sourdough starter — flour, water, and a colony of wild yeast that's been fed twice a day since a rainy week in 2015. He's been to two weddings, one house move, and has his own shelf in the walk-in.

We don't add commercial yeast or improvers. Just Gerald, stone-milled flour, salt, water, and a lot of waiting. That's why the bread tastes like it does, and why there's only so much of it.
