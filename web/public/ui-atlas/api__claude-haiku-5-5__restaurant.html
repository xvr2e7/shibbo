Our Story

A kitchen built on memory and patience

In 1987, Layla and Youssef Haddad opened a small room with six tables and a single grill out back. They had carried their recipes from a village in the mountains above Beirut, folded into suitcases and memory.

Today, their daughter Nadia runs the kitchen and their grandson Karim handles the wood-fired grill. Everything is still made by hand, every morning: the bread, the tahini, the pickles, and the kibbeh that takes two people half a day to shape.

We source spices from a family supplier in Tripoli, olive oil from a grove in Kfardebian, and produce from farms within an hour of our door. Our doors are open to everyone. Pull up a chair.

— The Haddad Family
