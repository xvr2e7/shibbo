Dr. Eleanor Rivers

Hydrologist & Writer on the Flow of Water

About

Dr. Eleanor Rivers is a hydrologist with over fifteen years of experience studying the world's waterways. She holds a PhD in Environmental Science from the University of Colorado and has conducted fieldwork on six continents, from the Amazon Basin to the Mekong Delta.

Her work bridges the technical and the poetic, exploring how rivers shape landscapes, cultures, and human history. When not analyzing stream flow data or conducting watershed assessments, Eleanor writes essays that bring the hidden lives of rivers to general audiences.

She currently serves as a senior researcher at the Global Water Institute and lives in Portland, Oregon, near the confluence of the Willamette and Columbia Rivers.

Essays

The Memory of Meanders

December 2023

Rivers remember their old paths. Even after being channelized or diverted, evidence of ancient meanders persists in soil composition, vegetation patterns, and the dreams of the land itself. This essay explores how rivers write their history into the landscape and what we lose when we try to erase that record.

Braided Rivers and Complexity

September 2023

In New Zealand's Canterbury Plains, glacial rivers split and rejoin in an endless dance of division and confluence. Braided rivers teach us about complexity, resilience, and the beauty of systems that refuse single narratives. A meditation on multiplicity in nature and life.

The Colorado River's Missing Water

June 2023

The Colorado River no longer reaches the sea. This essay traces the river's disappearance through the American Southwest, examining the human decisions that transformed one of North America's mightiest rivers into a series of engineered puddles—and what this means for our relationship with water in an era of scarcity.

Listening to Spring Melt

March 2023

Every spring, mountain snowpack transforms into rushing water, carrying winter's stored wealth to the lowlands. By installing hydrophones in Rocky Mountain streams, I discovered that spring melt has a sound—a complex symphony of ice, stone, and liquid motion that reveals the river's hidden rhythms.

Underground Rivers and Hidden Worlds

January 2023

Beneath our feet flow rivers we never see. Karst aquifers and subterranean streams carve vast caverns in limestone, creating ecosystems as complex as any rainforest. This essay descends into the darkness to explore what rivers become when they flow beyond the reach of sunlight.

The Jordan River: Water and Meaning

October 2022

Few rivers carry as much symbolic weight as the Jordan. Once a robust stream flowing through the Levant, it now struggles under the demands of multiple nations and competing narratives. An exploration of how rivers become metaphors and what happens when the physical reality can no longer support the meaning we've assigned.

Salmon Highways and the Architecture of Return

August 2022

Pacific salmon transform rivers into highways of determination, traveling thousands of miles to spawn in the streams where they were born. Their journey reveals the deep architecture of river systems and raises profound questions about home, memory, and the meaning of return.

Dam Removal and the Practice of Letting Go

May 2022

After decades of controlling rivers, we're learning to let them go. Dam removal projects across North America show us what rivers do when given freedom: they remember, reshape, and regenerate. This essay follows the Elwha River's return to wildness after the removal of two major dams.
