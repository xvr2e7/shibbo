About

I'm a hydrologist who studies the movement of water through landscapes and the stories rivers tell about our changing world. After fifteen years researching fluvial systems from the Amazon to the Yangtze, I've learned that understanding rivers means understanding the deep time of geology, the quick time of weather, and the human time of decision-making.

I write to make sense of what I see in the field—the way a river remembers its floods, the trade-offs embedded in every dam, the quiet politics of snowmelt. These essays explore the science, history, and ethics of water in an era of scarcity and change.
