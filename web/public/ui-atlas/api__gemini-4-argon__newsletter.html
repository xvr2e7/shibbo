ISSUE #142 // STREET SECTION ANALYSIS

The 20-Meter Street Diet: Reclaiming Asphalt for BRT & Bioswales

DATE: OCT 2024
SCALE: 1:100 METRIC

For sixty years, municipal traffic manuals treated the 20-meter urban arterial as a throughput pipe: four 3.5-meter travel lanes flanked by two parking lanes and narrow 1.5-meter sidewalks. The result is familiar—high-speed conflicts, zero tree canopy, and buses trapped in single-occupancy congestion.

By narrowing general travel lanes to 3.0 meters and consolidating curb parking into alternating loading bays, cities from Utrecht to Bogotá have unlocked 6.5 meters of right-of-way without moving a single building facade. That reclaimed width is enough for physically separated cycle tracks and center-running Bus Rapid Transit.
